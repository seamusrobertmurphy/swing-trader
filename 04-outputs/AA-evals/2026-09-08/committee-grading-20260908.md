# Grading the committee's August ratings (08 September 2026)

Eight ratings were produced on 16 August 2026 and never scored. This is the first scoring, 23 days later, against Binance daily closes.

Three questions are asked separately. **Direction** asks whether a coin the committee wanted less of actually fell. **Selection** asks whether it underperformed bitcoin, which is the benchmark the wrapper configures and the only question that matters to a book that must hold something. **Spread** asks whether the ratings distinguished between coins at all, because a set of identical ratings carries no information however accurate it is.

## The score

- **Direction: 0 of 7.** The committee asked for less of 7 coins. 0 of them fell.
- **Selection: 1 of 7.** 1 of them underperformed bitcoin.
- **Price targets: 0 of 8.** Price moved toward the stated target in 0 cases and away from it in 8.

**What listening would have cost.** An equal-weight basket of the 7 distinct coins, simply held, returned +34.38 per cent over the window. Trimming each position as the committee asked, 30 per cent on Underweight and a full exit on Sell, returned +21.55 per cent. Following the committee cost -12.83 percentage points in 23 days, before any fee for making the trims.

## Caveats that would change the reading

1. Eight ratings on one day is not a sample. Nothing here is a verdict on the framework; it decides whether the layer earns more API spend.
2. Several stated horizons have not elapsed. The bitcoin call was written for two to four months and has had 23 days. The Solana call, at two to four weeks, is the only one comfortably inside its own window.
3. Two entries rate the same asset. `PEPE-USD` and `PEPE24478-USD` are both PEPE, the second being a Yahoo ticker-collision override, and the committee gave them different ratings in the same run.
4. No fee is charged here because no trade was placed. Any real use of these ratings would pay the same round trip as everything else, which is the bar that has killed every other crypto candidate.
