The bull argues: *"RSI at 42 is an ideal entry point for contrarian bulls—it's weak enough to be unpopular, strong enough to have room to run."*

### RSI is a *Momentum* Indicator, Not a *Mean Reversion* Indicator

The bull frames RSI at 42 as "neutral with recovery potential." But let's look at what RSI *actually* measures:

**RSI = Relative Strength Index = (14-period average gain) / (14-period average loss)**

An RSI of 42 doesn't mean "price is weak but will bounce." It means **the ratio of winning days to losing days over the past 14 periods favors sellers.**

### The Real Signal in the RSI Data

The bull cites that RSI has "bounced between 37–57 over the 30-day period," framing this as "healthy volatility." But let's examine the *direction*:

- **July 21 (local peak at $0.77):** RSI = 56.98 (near overbought)

**This is not a "recovery signal." This is a *deterioration signal*.**

The fact that RSI *peaked* at the same time price peaked (July 21) and has been *declining* since then indicates that **momentum is waning as price falls.** This is the classic setup for continued weakness, not reversal.

**Contrarian principle:** RSI is only a true contrarian signal when it reaches extremes (>70 or <30). At 42, RSI is simply reflecting the current bearish bias. There's nothing contrarian about it.

### The "Hasn't Capitulated" Argument Backfires

The bull argues: *"The fact that RSI has recovered from lows (didn't capitulate to 20–30 range) suggests no panic selling."*

**Wrong.** This actually suggests that sellers are *in control*. Here's why:

If there were panic selling (capitulation at RSI <20), you'd typically see:

**This pattern is consistent with controlled distribution, not capitulation.** It's *worse* than panic selling because it indicates orderly selling—exactly what you'd expect if informed traders (insiders, early investors) are gradually exiting positions.

The bull has it backwards: **The lack of capitulation signals suggests the selling is NOT done yet.**

1. **Volume *increases* (not decreases)** because institutional trades are large and require significant liquidity

- **Price is declining** — NOT holding support or rising
