Reading. All five regimes that ignore time claimed less error than the two
that keep it, in the direction a leak predicts, and leave-one-out claimed a
cross-validated error below the training error, which is impossible without
seeing the answer. The effect is small, 0.002 to 0.016, and the record itself
says the design does not clear its own noise, because leave-one-out scores 200
random rows per seed and its spread across seeds is 0.0177. The reason the leak
is small is the model, not the regime: a depth-4 forest with 200 rows a leaf
cannot memorise a neighbouring row, so it cannot leak one. That is why the
`regime-memoriser` design exists, the same seven regimes on a forest with
unconstrained depth and one row a leaf, which had a walk-forward overfit ratio
of 3.68 on 8 September. Its record is the decisive one and it is in the
next section.

## Next stages of testing

In the order they should run, with what each decides.

1. Read the memoriser and estimator records and write the findings into
   `.claude/memory/general.md` and the CLAUDE.md build log. The memoriser row
   answers whether a random partition can pass the overfit bar for a model that
   walk-forward rejects at 3.68. The estimator row answers whether any learner
   other than the forest sits in the usable corner, ratio under 1.1 and blind
   U2 under one, on the same rows.
2. Purge between walk-forward folds. `bench_run.folds_of` places no gap between
   a fold's training rows and its scored rows, while the label looks twelve
   bars ahead, so the last twelve training rows of every fold carry outcomes
   from the scored block. The blind cut is embargoed; the folds are not. Add a
   `purge_bars` field to the split section, default the label horizon, and sweep
   0, 12 and 24. Expected to move the walk-forward claimed error up by a little
   and to be the honest baseline the other regimes are measured against.
3. The regime sweep at scale, once on the full four-hour panel at a 40,000-row
   cap and once on the equity daily panel (`market equity, frame eq1d`). Both
   need the 32 GB machine; this one swaps at a 25 MB slice. The question is
   whether the leak grows with rows, as it should if it comes from neighbouring
   bars.
4. The estimator sweep under both class weights. Weight was the largest axis in
   the September digest (0.0258 against a mean configuration span of 0.0298),
   and the estimator sweep ran unweighted only. Six estimators by two weights,
   three repeats, about ten minutes.
5. A label-geometry design. The label section (target, stop, horizon in ATR and
   bars) is in the bench configuration and has never been swept through it; the
   June sweep used a different script. Rows moving one axis at a time from
   +2/-1/12: +1.5, +3 on the target; -0.5, -2 on the stop; 6, 24, 48 on the
   horizon. This is the "target and the data, not the model" finding made into
   a sweep.
6. Cross-sectional relative strength on equities with a longer hold. The
   ranking signal was never disproved on crypto (25 of 42 signals sign-stable
   train to test) and lost only because the whole market fell; the two levers
   never tried are a longer hold and a market that drifts up. The momentum
   harness in `equity_momentum_monthly.py` already does non-overlapping monthly
   holds and is the frame to reuse.
7. The momentum re-test on fresh bars. Last run 5 September (monthly spread
   +1.006%/month, t 2.41; weekly +0.245%/week, t 2.33; the 2026H2 fold the worst
   selection half-year on record). Rerun after the sixth rebalance on 21
   September, when the execution verdict is due: six cycles, measured cost
   against the 5 to 10 basis point model.
