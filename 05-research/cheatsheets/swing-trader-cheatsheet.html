A1The fee wallthe whole story

Every trade pays a toll in and another out, so a faster clock pays more often. The 5-minute frame has an edge before fees; the toll is six times it.

This is the wall  A basis point is a hundredth of one per cent. The best crypto edge is five to twelve of them smaller than the fifteen to twenty a round trip costs. Nothing tried has closed it.

A2What counts as a winthree barriers

A win is a decision someone makes, not a fact waiting to be found. Three lines are drawn from the moment of buying: a profit to aim at, a loss to bail out at, and a deadline. Whichever the price touches first is the answer.

A target can lose before the model speaks  On the 4-hour panel the profit line is reached first on 254 bars in every thousand. Aiming twice as far up as down, you need 333 to come out level. The arithmetic is losing before anything is fitted, so the first thing to check is always the target's own record, not the model's.

A3Passing the barfolds, not totals

A grand total can be carried by one lucky year. So history is cut into half-year blocks and the edge has to show up in at least 60 per cent of them, on two questions at once.

QuestionWhat it asks
Did it paydid the picks make money after fees
Did it beatdid they beat the average name in the same block
Never read the average alone  Those five blocks average out to exactly what a steady 24.9 would, and a steady 24.9 would be a usable model. These are not.

B2Getting into the universeall four or out

Four doors, and a coin has to pass all four before it is even considered. The test is re-run on every bar using only what was known on that day, because a filter applied with hindsight is not a filter.

DoorBar it has to clear
Trading enough≥ 30m USDT in 24h
Moving enoughATR 2.5–12% of price
Cheap to cross≤ 0.5% estimated
Old enough≥ 120 days lived

ATR, the average true range, is how far price usually travels in a day, as a share of price. The band shown is daily; a 4-hour bar wants 1.0 to 4.9%.

The spread, the gap between what buyers offer and sellers ask, is paid both ways. The archive holds no order book, so it is estimated from each day's high and low and gated at 0.5%.

Rank wide, buy narrow  The whole market is scored, then only coins that actually carried the edge on unseen data are allowed through at the moment of buying.

B3Rank, do not predictthe surviving idea

Two different questions, and only one of them has ever produced anything. Will this coin go up? failed on every frame. Which of these coins is strongest right now? did not.

Two claims, kept apart  The sorting works and has never been disproved. The one way of trading it that was built, waiting for bitcoin to trend up first, held in 27 per cent of blocks against the 60 the rule wants, and was killed. Only the second sentence is settled. Untried: holding longer to pay the toll less often, and running the same sort on shares, where the market drifts up.
CWhat the model readssignals, and which ones earn their place

C1What the model readsfour groups

Twenty-four numbers do the work of ninety  Asked how many bars a trend has left, the 90 built inputs miss by 24.213 bars on the 4-hour panel. Twenty-four plain past returns, nothing built at all, miss by 24.247. Guessing the average misses by 26.477. The two real answers sit a thirty-fourth of a bar apart, so they hold the same information and a memory-based model has no gap left to close.

DHow it is fittedsplits, tuning, training

D1Splitting timenever shuffled

Shuffle the rows and you put next Tuesday in the study pile, then test on last Monday. Prices carry over from one bar to the next, so a shuffle lets the future leak backwards and every score comes out flattering.

The yellow sliver is the gap. Without it the last rows studied still await outcomes that land inside the test window, the same leak in a different hat.

Once means once  The final year is opened at the end and scored a single time. A setting chosen by looking at it has spent it, and the year cannot be made unseen again.

D2What tuning showedsmaller, not bigger

Nine settings, each tried on three separate stretches of the 4-hour panel, all asked the same question: how many bars until the trend turns?

When beats how  Two experiments agree. The nine settings above span 1.366 bars end to end; a separate test of three ways of feeding the model spanned 0.094. Move one model from a calm stretch of market to a rough one and it shifts 3.83. Which months you test on moves the score further than any choice you make about the model.

Two faults, both in the judging  The winner was chosen on the unseen miss alone, never the ratio, crowning HistGBM at 1.840 where the rule rejects past 1.1. And every model treats rare wins as common, pushing its probabilities away from what this score measures: on 4-hour bars it misses by 0.4844 where answering "25.4 per cent, always" misses 0.4353. Nothing checks whether a stated 30 per cent happens 30 per cent of the time.

The one that works

Twelve-month share momentum. Buy what rose most over the past year, skip the latest month, hold. Over 116 months it beat the average share by 1.006 points a month, 2.41 times its own wobble, where two is the bar. It paid in 85 per cent of blocks and beat the market in 80.

Closed: 1-hour direction, a coin flip; sharper entries, 29% of blocks; the breadth gate, 27%.

Traps that cost daysevery one of these happened here, and each was found late

A number quoted from memory Three figures on the previous version of this sheet were wrong because they were recalled rather than read. Open the file that produced the number, every time, including your own.

Rendering is not checking A page can build, exit cleanly, and report 0 of 6 cycles where there are 3. Compare the new render against the last one before believing it.

The same error, twice The overfit ratio was written upside down, corrected on 7 September in one file, and found the same day still inverted in a second. A model reported as stable at 0.902 was really 1.109, which the rule rejects.

A paper account is not a mark On 7 September, a market holiday, the broker reported $98,362 while the holdings at Friday's real closes came to $96,917. Value the positions yourself before reporting a number.
