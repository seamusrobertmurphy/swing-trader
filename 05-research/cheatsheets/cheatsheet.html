Control Centre

Six panels that set up, run and score one test of a trading model on past prices, read left to right. Each saves its own settings, the Scoreboard runs the test with all of them, and every result is kept with the settings that made it.

Does any rule make money after trading costs on a year of prices the model never saw?

Answer so far

Crypto, no. Of 120 random crypto test setups run on 24 September, 9 made money after costs once overlapping trades were removed, and hourly, 4-hour and daily bars all lost at the median.

US stocks, mostly the market. Daily stock setups made money in 43 of 45, but buying every stock made money in all 45, a median 0.71 per cent a trade, and the model's picks added a median of only 0.18.

One strategy passed. 12-1 momentum on US stocks cleared every test and has run on a paper account since 18 August.

Testing an idea

1 Change one thingChange one setting on its panel and save it. Two changes at once cannot be told apart.

2 Run the testPress Run the test on C1, which uses every saved setting.

3 Read the marksTest U2 below 1, RMSE ratio at or under 1.1, and for the three-way outcome, money after cost.

4 CompareAgainst the run before. A change in U2 smaller than 0.0062 is within the spread of repeats.

5 Keep or revertKeep what beat the record. Never choose a setting by looking at the test year, because it has then been seen.

Terms

ATRAverage true range, the typical high-to-low move over 14 bars.

Triple barrierA trade ends at the take-profit, the stop or the time limit, whichever comes first.

Base rateThe share of bars where the take-profit came first.

Test yearThe final 365 days, held back and scored once, the board's blind period.

FoldOne cut of the training years, fitted on part and scored on the rest.

Walk-forwardFit on the past, score the stretch after it, then move forward in time.

Theil's U2The model's error over that of always forecasting the base rate. Below 1 beats it.

RMSERoot mean squared error. Held-out over training RMSE is the RMSE ratio, or overfit ratio.

Top fifthThe fifth of bars the model rated most likely to rise.

Brier scoreThe mean squared error of a stated probability.

MACDA fast moving average of price minus a slow one.

Gradient boostingTrees fitted in turn, each to the last one's errors. HistGBM and LightGBM are two kinds.

Basis pointOne hundredth of a per cent, written bp.

Paper accountReal prices and simulated orders, with no money at risk.

Using the App

The public page at seamusrobertmurphy.github.io/swing-trader. Anyone sets up a model, runs it on fresh prices and gets paper trades settled on real prices, with no keys, no code and no money.

How do I run a model, change it, and read the trades it makes?

1StartQuick start, home page and A1

A model runs from a phone in about five minutes, and nothing is bought or sold.

  1. Tap Binance for crypto or Alpaca for US stocks.
  2. Tap a preset, which fills every setting on every panel.
  3. Type your name and press Run Model, on the home page, B2 or C1.
  4. Open C2 for your paper trades. Each is settled on real prices when its time is up.

3Forecast charttrades on candles, home page and C2

Shows each preset's own paper trades on recent candles. Pick Binance or Alpaca, a preset, then a symbol tab.

  1. Candles, green when the close beat the open, red when not.
  2. Three Supertrend bands, trailing stops under the price in an uptrend; a line breaks where its band flips.
  3. Agreement strip, how many bands point up, red for none to green for all three.
  4. Blue shading, the 24 candles before a taken trade.
  5. Green triangle, a trade taken; a grey ring is one skipped.
  6. Take-profit in green dashes, stop in red.
  7. A cross where it closed, green at take-profit, red at stop, grey at the time limit.
  8. Trailing stop, in orange, when its button is on.
  9. Gold fan, TimesFM's forecast now, 10th to 90th percentile; blue fans are its forecasts at taken trades.
  10. Volume, green on an up candle.
  11. Lower panel, MACD or RSI with its 70 and 30 zones, and each taken trade's reading.
4Exitslower panel and exit tools

Lower panel

MACD by default, a fast average of the price minus a slow one. RSI switches to the 14-candle relative strength index, 0 to 100, above 70 after a fast rise and below 30 after a fast fall, with each taken trade's RSI marked.

Trailing stop

Replays the research's exit on every taken trade, a stop two volatility widths under each close that only steps up and a take-profit that shrinks to the entry price by the time limit. A diamond marks where it would have closed.

Exit shapes

Under the table, one taken trade closed three ways side by side, fixed, trailing, and trailing with a shrinking take-profit. Touch a green triangle to choose it.

The table

Under the chart, it sets taken trades against skipped ones on result, exit reason, bands up at entry, run-up, volume and RSI, and the trailing exit against the fixed one.

Touch any markEvery candle, trade and line gives its numbers. How to read it opens the full note, and Key the legend.

When it updates

  1. Scans every four hours, at 23 minutes past.
  2. Due trades settled hourly, at 17 minutes past.
  3. Forecasts and boards rebuilt daily from 00:30 UTC.
  4. Old page history wiped on the 1st of each month, current files kept.
6ResultsC2, your trades and the scoreboard

Paper trades lists every user's runs, newest first, yours highlighted, under three charts.

Taken against skipped plots the running total after cost, so the gap between its lines is the edge. Model scores gives each run's RMSE, MAE, Theil's U2 or overfit ratio, and What carries the book the total by model or symbol.

Each trade's Why this call gives its score group, expected move and which of the four checks passed.

Revising a design

1 Start from a presetTap the nearest preset so every other setting is known.

2 Change one thingOne setting on A1 or under Advanced. Two changes at once cannot be told apart.

3 Run ModelAbout five minutes. Your name marks your trades on C2.

4 Wait for settlingCrypto 4-hour trades close in 2 days, stock trades in 20 trading days. An open trade has no result.

5 CompareTaken trades should beat skipped ones, and the weekly scoreboard sets the presets and trials on the same weeks.

Terms

Paper tradeA trade recorded at a real price with no money, also called a ticket.

SettledClosed at its take-profit, stop or time limit, with a result after cost.

Taken, skippedWhether the model's call passed all four checks.

Supertrend bandA trailing stop that flips side when the trend turns.

Run-upThe price move over the 24 candles before an entry.

RSIRelative strength index, recent gains against losses, 0 to 100.

TimesFMGoogle's forecasting model, drawn as a fan and never traded on.

Trailing stopA stop that follows the price up and never down.

Shrinking take-profitA target that falls to the entry price by the time limit.

Cost floorDrops the costliest fifth of candles to trade.

Edge floorThe least expected move after cost a taken trade needs.

Trial strategyBest on record with one change, a shorter hold, a looser buy level or a trend filter.

Weekly scoreboardEach strategy's average taken trade by the week it closed.

Confirmed-best rule30 independent trades with an interval above zero, fixed before any settled.
